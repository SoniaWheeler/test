🧩 Lesson 3 – Designing Data Solutions

Generate, compare and select a suitable design for a data investigation.

🎯 Learning Intention

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to create different possible designs for a data solution, compare them against requirements, and select a suitable design.

📖 Lesson

Work through each section in order. Click a section to see the learning content and activity.

Section 1 of 4

🎯 Understanding Requirements

Before designing a solution, we need to know what it needs to achieve.

📋 What are Requirements?

Requirements describe what your data solution needs to achieve. They help you decide whether a design is suitable for the problem.

Requirement Why is it important?
Relevant data The data helps answer the question.
Enough data There is enough information to identify patterns.
Practical The solution can be completed with the available time and resources.
Clear results The findings can be easily understood.
💡 Key idea

Requirements give you a way to judge whether a possible solution is suitable.

1 Activity – Identify Requirements

Your school wants to investigate whether the amount of recreational screen time students have is related to how much sleep they get.

Identify three requirements for the data solution.

  1. One requirement about the data.
  2. One requirement about collecting the data.
  3. One requirement about presenting the results.

🧩 Creating Alternative Designs

There is often more than one way to solve a data problem.

💡 Why Create Alternative Designs?

Designers do not always use their first idea. Creating alternative designs gives you different possible ways to solve the same problem.

The designs can then be compared against the requirements before a final choice is made.

Design A
One possible approach
Design B
A different approach
Design C
Another possibility
💡 Remember

Your alternative designs should be meaningfully different. Changing only a small detail does not create a genuinely different solution.

2 Activity – Create Two Designs

Use the screen-time and sleep investigation from Activity 1.

Create two different designs for collecting, analysing and presenting the data.

For each design, consider:

  • What data will you collect?
  • Who will provide the data?
  • How will you collect it?
  • How will you analyse it?
  • How will you present the results?

🔎 Comparing Designs

Evaluate each possible design against the requirements.

🔎 How Do We Compare Designs?

Once you have created different designs, compare each design against the requirements.

This process is called evaluating a design against the requirements.

Ask yourself:

  • Does the design collect the data I need?
  • Can I realistically complete it?
  • Will the results be useful?
  • Can the results be presented clearly?
💡 Example

One requirement might be: "The data collection must be completed within one school lesson."

A design that requires students to collect data over seven days would not meet this requirement.

3 Activity – Compare Your Designs

Compare the two designs you created in Activity 2 against your requirements.

For each requirement, decide whether each design:

  • ✅ Meets the requirement
  • ⚠️ Partly meets the requirement
  • ❌ Does not meet the requirement

Explain why you made each decision.

🏆 Selecting a Preferred Design

Use your comparison to choose a suitable solution.

🏆 How Do We Select a Design?

After comparing your alternative designs, select the design that best meets the requirements.

Your decision should be based on the evidence from your comparison rather than simply choosing your favourite idea.

🗣️ Sentence starter

I selected Design ___ because it meets the requirement of ___ and will allow me to ___.

4 Activity – Select Your Preferred Design

Look at your comparison from Activity 3.

Select the design that best meets your requirements.

Explain your choice using evidence from your comparison.

Try using this structure:

I selected Design ___ because it meets the requirement of ___. It is suitable because ___. However, one limitation is ___.

⭐ Final thought

A preferred design is not necessarily the most complicated design. It is the design that is most suitable for the problem and meets the requirements.

🚀 Key Takeaways

Generate → Compare → Select → Justify

Good data solutions are designed by considering different possibilities, checking them against requirements, and selecting a suitable approach.
